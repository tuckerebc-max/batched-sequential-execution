# "The Last Utility" — manuscript structure and excerpt (fictional, abridged)

The full manuscript is approximately 30,000 words across a preface, nine
chapters, and back matter. Reproduced here is the chapter structure and a
short representative excerpt from each region, sufficient for a unit's
brief without pasting the entire book into this example. Word counts given
are the current (pre-revision) chapter lengths, used by acceptance
criterion 4 (revised length within 10% of current length).

## Preface & Introduction (~1,900 words)
Sets up the book's thesis: that municipal broadband fights are really
fights about who gets to define "public works" in an era when the wire
matters as much as the pipe. Introduces the book's four recurring
case-study towns.

Excerpt: "Harlow County did not set out to become a fight about federal
funding formulas. It set out to get its kids off a school bus that idled
in a gravel lot each afternoon because that was the only place three bars
of signal reliably showed up."

## Chapter 1 — Why Towns Built Their Own Wires (~3,100 words)
History chapter: rural electrification cooperatives as the template
municipal broadband advocates reach for, and where the analogy holds and
breaks down.

## Chapter 2 — The Incumbent Playbook (~3,400 words)
2.1 Origins: how incumbent telecom operators responded to the first wave
of municipal fiber proposals. 2.2 The Vote: a composite account of a
statehouse floor fight over a preemption bill.

## Chapter 3 — Case Study: Harlow County's Fiber Cooperative (~3,600 words)
3.1 The Cooperative's Founding: how a rural electric cooperative pivoted
into fiber. 3.2 Financing and Attribution: how the cooperative's
build-out was financed, and interviews with the county officials who
championed it, including its former finance director.

## Chapter 4 — Case Study: Cedar Bend's Failed Bond Measure (~2,900 words)
A composite account of a municipal bond measure that failed at referendum,
and what its failure reveals about ratepayer skepticism.

## Chapter 5 — The Federal Funding Maze (~3,300 words)
5.1 The Federal Program: a fictional "Rural Connectivity Fund" and its
application process. 5.2 State Matching Funds: how states layered their
own matching-fund programs on top of the federal one.

## Chapter 6 — Ratepayer Politics and Public Trust (~2,800 words)
6.1 Rate Design Fights: how municipal utilities set rates for a new fiber
utility alongside an existing electric utility. 6.2 Public Meetings
Backlash: composite accounts of contentious public hearings.

## Chapter 7 — Case Study: Fenwick Falls' Municipal Takeover (~3,500 words)
7.1 The Takeover Vote: a town's vote to acquire an aging cable
incumbent's local plant. 7.2 Aftermath: the first eighteen months of
municipal operation.

## Chapter 8 — What Comes After Federal Money Runs Out (~2,300 words)
Analysis chapter on post-grant sustainability, drawing on all four case
studies.

## Chapter 9 & Conclusion — Lessons for the Next Decade (~2,400 words)
Synthesizes the case studies into a set of practical lessons for other
towns considering the same path.

## Back matter — Acknowledgments & Appendix (~700 words)
Acknowledgments, interview subject list, and methodology note.
