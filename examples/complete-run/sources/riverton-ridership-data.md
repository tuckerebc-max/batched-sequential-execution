# Data memo — RTA ridership and fare revenue by time period

Prepared by RTA Planning & Scheduling from the FY2026 automated fare
collection (AFC) extract and the FY2026 operating budget. Figures cover
Riverton Transit Authority's fixed-route bus and light-rail network only;
paratransit (RTA Access) is reported separately in the stakeholder notes.

## System totals, FY2026

RTA recorded 21.4 million unweighted boardings in FY2026, up 1.8% from
FY2025. Fixed-route fare revenue was $28.6 million. RTA's existing fare
product is a single all-day cap: a rider pays full fare for their first 2
taps in a service day (6:00 a.m.-11:59 p.m.) and rides free the rest of that
day, regardless of when those first 2 taps occur. This all-day cap has been
in place since 2019 and is not time-of-day differentiated.

## Boardings and revenue by day-part

RTA's AFC system buckets boardings into three day-parts: AM peak (6:00-9:30
a.m.), midday/PM peak combined as "daytime" (9:30 a.m.-6:30 p.m., which
includes the 4:00-6:30 p.m. PM peak), and off-peak (6:30 p.m.-11:59 p.m. plus
all weekend hours). For FY2026:

- AM peak: 6.2 million boardings (29% of system total), $9.1 million revenue.
- Daytime (including PM peak): 10.5 million boardings (49% of system total),
  $13.8 million revenue.
- Off-peak (evenings and weekends): 4.7 million boardings (22% of system
  total), $5.7 million revenue.

Average fare revenue per boarding is therefore highest in AM peak ($1.47)
and lowest off-peak ($1.21), consistent with the existing all-day cap: a
rider who taps twice in the morning rush already rides free for the rest of
the day, including any off-peak trips, so off-peak boardings by cap-covered
riders generate no incremental revenue at all. RTA Planning estimates 38% of
off-peak boardings are made by riders who already reached the all-day cap
earlier that same day; the remaining 62% are riders making an off-peak-only
trip (a single fare, no cap benefit) or a second trip on a day when their
first trip was also off-peak.

## Growth and capacity

Off-peak ridership grew 3.1% year over year, faster than the 1.8% system
average, driven mostly by evening service on the two light-rail lines. RTA's
fleet utilization data shows off-peak trips run at 34% average seat
occupancy system-wide, compared with 78% in AM peak and 71% in PM peak — RTA
has substantial unused off-peak capacity on nearly every route. No route in
the FY2026 schedule is capacity-constrained off-peak.

## Prior fare-change history

RTA's board last changed the base fare in 2021 (a $0.15 increase) and
introduced the current all-day cap in 2019. Both changes went through a
90-day public comment period under RTA's fare-change policy. RTA has not
previously implemented a fare product differentiated by time of day; the
all-day cap is uniform regardless of when a rider's first two taps occur.
