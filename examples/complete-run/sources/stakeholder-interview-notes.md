# Stakeholder interview notes — off-peak fare cap proposal

Compiled by RTA Planning from three interviews conducted 2026-08 as part of
scoping an off-peak fare cap proposal. Notes are summarized from recordings
with each interviewee's consent; quotes are lightly edited for length.

## Amalgamated Transit Operators Local 219 (driver union), interview 2026-08-11

Interviewed: union grievance officer and one shop steward. The union's
primary concern was fare enforcement during any transition period, not the
policy itself. Quote: "Every time fare rules change, drivers are the ones
riders argue with at the farebox until the system catches up. Give us
written guidance and enough lead time before the change hits the road." The
union asked for a minimum 60-day advance notice before any fare change takes
effect on vehicles, citing Meridian Transit's six-week notice as a floor, not
a ceiling, given RTA's larger fleet. The union did not oppose an off-peak cap
in principle and noted that reduced disputes over an already-reached cap
(riders arguing they should ride free) would, if anything, reduce
farebox-adjacent conflict during off-peak shifts, which the union's members
generally staff.

## RTA Access (paratransit office), interview 2026-08-13

Interviewed: RTA Access program manager. RTA Access, the agency's paratransit
service, operates under a separate fare schedule (currently a flat $2.50 per
trip, not subject to the fixed-route all-day cap) and the program manager
was clear that any fixed-route off-peak cap should not be assumed to apply to
Access trips without a separate board action, since Access is funded and
budgeted on a distinct formula tied to ADA paratransit complementary-service
requirements. The program manager flagged one adjacent concern: if an
off-peak fixed-route cap meaningfully increases off-peak fixed-route
ridership among riders who currently use Access because fixed-route service
feels financially unpredictable off-peak, Access could see a small ridership
shift onto fixed-route, which the office would view as a positive outcome
for program cost but asked to be consulted before any public communication
implies Access riders are a target population for the fixed-route change.

## RTA Finance office, interview 2026-08-14

Interviewed: deputy CFO. Finance's central concern was cash-flow timing
under the real-time-capping option described in the fare-system technical
memo: a $410,000 up-front technology cost against a revenue effect that, per
Finance's read of the peer-agency data, is likely to be revenue-neutral to
modestly positive within a year but is not free in year one. The deputy CFO
was explicit that the office does not oppose the concept and thinks a phased
rollout, starting on RTA's highest reduced-fare-concentration corridors
before a system-wide expansion, is "the fiscally disciplined way to test the
revenue assumption before committing the full technology spend" — closer to
Ashford Regional Transit's corridor-first approach than to Meridian's
system-wide launch, though Finance also flagged that Ashford's low
rider-awareness result (only about a third of riders knew the cap existed by
month six) is a communication failure worth designing around, not a reason
to avoid a phased approach. Finance asked that any board memo distinguish
clearly between the $410,000 real-time option and the $85,000 interim
batch-credit option, since the two have materially different cash-flow and
procurement timelines.
