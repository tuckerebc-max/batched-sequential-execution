# Source memo — Off-peak fare capping: three peer-agency outcomes

Prepared by RTA Strategy & Planning, compiled from peer-agency public board
reports and direct agency-to-agency data requests, 2026-08. Three mid-size
agencies with comparable service area and fleet size to Riverton Transit
Authority (RTA) have implemented some form of off-peak fare capping. Figures
below are as each agency reported them; none have been independently audited
by RTA.

## Meridian Transit (population served ~310,000)

Launched a system-wide off-peak fare cap in March 2023: riders who tap 6 times
between 9:00 a.m.-3:00 p.m. or after 7:00 p.m. on a service day pay nothing
further that day, versus a 12-tap cap for peak-period riders. Meridian
reports off-peak boardings rose 11% in the twelve months after launch,
against 2% system-wide growth in the same window. Off-peak fare revenue per
boarding fell 9%, but total off-peak revenue rose 4% on the higher volume.
Meridian's finance office attributes most of the ridership gain to riders who
previously avoided a second midday trip once they had already paid a peak
fare that morning. Implementation cost was $340,000 in fare-system software
changes and took 7 months from board approval to launch. Meridian did not
change its fare enforcement or verification process; the cap is applied
automatically by its account-based fare system.

## Cedar Falls Regional Transit (population served ~265,000)

Launched an off-peak cap in September 2022, restricted to riders enrolled in
its reduced-fare program (seniors, riders with disabilities, and
income-qualified riders). Enrolled off-peak riders pay a flat $0.75 per
boarding after their second off-peak tap in a day, down from a per-boarding
fare of $1.25. Cedar Falls reports enrolled-rider off-peak boardings rose 18%
in the first year, and cites exit-survey data showing 61% of enrolled riders
who increased off-peak travel cited the cap as the reason. Because the
program targeted only enrolled riders, total system revenue impact was small:
a 0.6% reduction in system-wide fare revenue. Cedar Falls' technology cost was
lower, $95,000, because the cap only had to be evaluated against one rider
class already flagged in the fare system; the tradeoff its board minutes note
explicitly is that non-enrolled low-income riders who have not completed
reduced-fare enrollment see no benefit at all.

## Ashford Regional Transit (population served ~220,000)

Ran a 14-month pilot, March 2024 through May 2025, on a single high-ridership
corridor rather than system-wide, capping off-peak boardings at 4 taps per
day on that corridor only. Ashford reports corridor off-peak ridership rose
7%, a smaller gain than Meridian's system-wide result, which Ashford's
after-action report attributes to the pilot's single-corridor scope limiting
awareness — only 34% of surveyed riders on the corridor knew the cap existed
by month 6. Ashford's pilot cost $110,000 and took 5 months to stand up
because it reused existing fare-system logic rather than building new
account-based capping. Ashford's board voted in June 2025 to expand the cap
system-wide starting in 2027, citing the pilot's ridership gain as sufficient
evidence despite the low awareness rate.

## Cross-agency notes

All three agencies report that off-peak fare capping produced a ridership
gain without producing a full-fare-equivalent revenue loss, because the
riders responding to the cap were largely riders who would otherwise have
made zero or one off-peak trip, not riders substituting away from an
already-paid peak fare. None of the three agencies reported measurable
change in vehicle crowding or added service costs from the added off-peak
ridership; all three had spare off-peak capacity before launch. Meridian and
Cedar Falls both used their existing account-based (tap-and-cap) fare
systems with a software change; Ashford's corridor-only approach was a
lower-cost, lower-awareness path that its own board later judged
insufficient. No agency in this set reported a labor-relations dispute tied
to the fare change itself, though Meridian's operator union did request (and
receive) six weeks of advance notice before the fare change took effect on
vehicles.
