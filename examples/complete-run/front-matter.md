# Off-Peak Fare Capping at Riverton Transit Authority: A Briefing Note

## Executive Summary

Riverton Transit Authority (RTA) has run a single fare product since 2019: an
all-day cap that pays off after a rider's first two taps in a service day,
regardless of when those taps occur. That design rewards riders who commute
during the morning peak and then ride free the rest of the day; it does
nothing for a rider whose only trips are in the evening, at night, or on
weekends. This briefing asks whether RTA should add a second product — an
off-peak fare cap that pays off after fewer taps, but only for taps inside
the off-peak window — and, if so, how to roll one out.

The evidence points toward yes, phased rather than all at once. Three peer
agencies that added an off-peak cap each reported an off-peak ridership gain
without a full-fare-equivalent revenue loss, because the riders who
responded were largely riders making a trip they otherwise would not have
made, not riders substituting away from a peak fare they had already paid.
RTA's own data shows why that pattern would likely repeat here: 22% of RTA's
boardings happen off-peak, at RTA's lowest average revenue per boarding, on
routes running at less than half the seat occupancy of the morning peak.
RTA's Equity & Access office's analysis sharpens who stands to gain most: RTA's
reduced-fare riders are proportionally twice as likely to be traveling
off-peak as the ridership base overall, and the current all-day cap
structurally excludes exactly this group from any cap benefit at all.

Building a cap that treats time of day differently is not free. RTA's fare
system currently evaluates the existing cap overnight, not in real time, and
a genuine time-of-day product needs that evaluation moved to the moment of
the tap — a $410,000, nine-month change, against a lower-cost, lower-fidelity
$85,000, three-month alternative that settles the difference as an account
credit days later rather than at the farebox. Three stakeholders have
already weighed in: the driver union wants substantial advance notice before
any change reaches vehicles, RTA Access needs a separate board action before
anything extends to paratransit fares, and RTA's own Finance office favors
testing the revenue assumption on a smaller base before committing to the
larger technology spend.

Sections 1 through 5 lay out this evidence in full: what peer agencies
found, what RTA's own ridership and revenue data shows, who the current cap
underserves, what building a time-of-day cap would actually take, and three
concrete phasing options with their tradeoffs. The Conclusion states this
briefing's recommendation once that evidence is in front of the reader; no
section before it does.
