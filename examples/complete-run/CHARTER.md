# CHARTER — Off-Peak Fare Capping at Riverton Transit Authority: A Briefing Note

## Deliverable
What: A briefing note to the Riverton Transit Authority (RTA) Board of
Directors evaluating whether RTA should adopt an off-peak fare cap, and
recommending how. Through-line: RTA should adopt an off-peak fare cap
through a phased, corridor-first pilot, because peer-agency evidence shows
off-peak capping grows ridership without a full-fare-equivalent revenue
loss, and RTA's own ridership and equity data show the riders most
underserved by its current all-day cap are exactly the riders concentrated
in the off-peak window.
Format: markdown, rendered last via the format skill
Total size: 4200w, 5items
(body sections only, B002-B006; the executive summary is front-matter.md and
the conclusion/recommendation is back-matter.md, written by hand in the seam
pass — see PLAN.md and WALKTHROUGH.md — and are not separately budgeted plan
units)

## Through-line
RTA should adopt an off-peak fare cap through a phased, corridor-first pilot: peer-agency
evidence shows off-peak capping grows ridership without a full-fare-equivalent revenue loss,
and RTA's own data show the riders most underserved by its current all-day cap are exactly
the riders concentrated off-peak.

## Audience
The RTA Board of Directors and the General Manager's senior staff. They know
RTA's network and current fare structure well; they do not have the peer-
agency data, the fare-system technical constraints, or the equity office's
analysis in front of them, and this note is their first consolidated look at
all three together ahead of a vote on whether to direct staff to proceed.

## Voice & register
Third person, board-briefing register: precise, plain, comfortable citing
figures, and willing to name a tradeoff without hiding it. No first person,
no rhetorical questions, and no recommendation stated as settled until the
conclusion — body sections lay out evidence and tradeoffs, not verdicts.

## Non-negotiables
1. Every ridership, revenue, cost, or date figure carries a [S#] source tag
   traceable to a file in Sources of truth.
2. The terminology lock is followed exactly; no synonyms for locked terms.
3. The implementation options section presents at least two options as
   options the Board could choose among, never as a decision already made.
4. Equity implications for low-income riders are addressed as their own
   subject, not folded into a general accessibility statement.
5. No section before the conclusion states outright that RTA should adopt
   an off-peak cap; sections build the evidence, the conclusion states the
   recommendation.

## Sources of truth
- [S1] sources/peer-program-outcomes.md — three peer agencies' off-peak fare
  cap outcomes (Meridian Transit, Cedar Falls Regional Transit, Ashford
  Regional Transit)
- [S2] sources/riverton-ridership-data.md — RTA's FY2026 ridership and fare
  revenue by time of day, and its current all-day cap
- [S3] sources/farebox-tech-memo.md — RTA IT/Fare Systems memo on what the
  RiverPass fare system requires to support a time-of-day cap
- [S4] sources/equity-analysis-notes.md — RTA Equity & Access office analysis
  of off-peak ridership and low-income (reduced-fare) riders
- [S5] sources/stakeholder-interview-notes.md — interview notes with the
  driver union, RTA Access (paratransit), and RTA Finance

## Acceptance criteria
1. Body sections (B002-B006) total 4,200 words +/-20%, and the source
   catalog lists all five sources.
2. The section on RTA's own ridership data ties the existing all-day cap's
   mechanics to which riders benefit from it, using [S2].
3. The equity section states at least one quantified finding from [S4]
   about who is underserved by the current cap.
4. The operational/technical feasibility section distinguishes the
   real-time and interim batch-credit options from [S3], with their
   respective costs and timelines.
5. The implementation options section presents at least two phasing options
   without declaring either one the Board's decision.
6. The finished piece (final.md, body plus front matter and back matter) is
   comprehensible as a single document reading start to finish, and its
   conclusion's recommendation is supported by evidence already stated in
   the body, introducing no new claim.

## Out of scope
- Procurement process and vendor selection for any fare-system software
  change (a subsequent staff action, not a Board policy decision).
- Regional fare integration with adjacent-county transit systems.
- The RTA Access (paratransit) fare schedule itself, beyond noting that it
  is governed separately.
- Capital budget appropriation mechanics for any technology spend.
