# Source Catalog

This catalog lists the five sources of truth for this report, plus the
cross-source cautions a later unit needs before citing them. Each entry
gives a source's [S#] tag, what the document is, and which planned sections
rely on it. Later units read only the specific slices they need; this
catalog is the one place that summarizes all five in full.

## Sources

- [S1] sources/ferc-order-2222-summary.md — a regulatory-affairs summary
  memo describing Federal Order 2222-B and, specifically, what it does and
  does not change about retail interconnection authority. Feeds Section 2
  (Policy Landscape) and Section 3.3 (Technical Standards).

- [S2] sources/state-survey-2026.md — Cascadia Energy Policy Institute's
  2026 eighteen-state survey of battery energy storage system (BESS)
  interconnection timelines, including median days-to-permission-to-operate
  by system size and queue-deposit practices. Feeds Section 1 (Problem
  Statement), Section 3.1 (Queue Mechanics), and Appendix A.

- [S3] sources/utility-interconnection-timelines.md — utility-reported
  queue data from four named utilities, including active queue size and the
  median delay added by supplemental review. Feeds Section 3.1 (Queue
  Mechanics), Section 5.2 (Rate Design and Cost Recovery), and Appendix A.

- [S4] sources/case-studies-vermont-hawaii.md — two composite state case
  studies (a shared-queue-priority program and a hosting-capacity-mapping
  program), each with quantified results and a stated tradeoff. Feeds
  Section 4.1, Section 4.2, and Section 4.3 (Comparative Analysis).

- [S5] sources/cost-benefit-model-notes.md — a methodology memo documenting
  illustrative cost-allocation modeling assumptions (applicant-pays,
  socialized-through-rates, hybrid threshold). Feeds Section 3.2 (Cost
  Allocation Methods) and Section 5.1 (Model Legislation Options); every
  figure drawn from this source must be flagged as illustrative, not
  observed, per charter non-negotiable 4.

## Cross-source cautions for downstream units

- [S1] is the only source that speaks to federal/state authority division —
  do not infer that division from [S2] or [S3], which are both retail-only
  queue data with no federal comparison.

- [S5]'s dollar figures ($180,000 per constraint, 40 applicants) are
  sensitivity-testing assumptions for Section 5.1's modeling and must never
  be presented as an observed cost in the report body.

- [S4]'s program-cost figures ($3.1M build, $410K/year, both from the
  shared-queue-priority case) are the only observed cost-allocation data in
  the source set; Section 5.2 should prefer these over [S5] wherever both
  could apply.

- [S2] and [S3] describe different populations (a multi-state survey vs.
  four named utilities) and should never be averaged together into a single
  headline figure; Section 3.1 should cite them as two distinct data points.

- [S4]'s two case studies are composites, not literal named states; Section
  4.1 and 4.2 should describe them as "a shared-savings queue reform" and
  "a hosting-capacity-mapping reform," never assert a specific real state.
