# Summary memo — Federal Order 2222-B (fictional)

Prepared by Cascadia Energy Policy Institute, Regulatory Affairs Desk.

Federal Order 2222-B (issued 2024, effective for RTO/ISO compliance filings
through 2026) requires each regional transmission organization to accept
aggregations of distributed energy resources — rooftop solar, behind-the-meter
batteries, and controllable water heaters — as a single dispatchable resource
in wholesale markets, provided the aggregator can demonstrate telemetry at the
device level. [S1]

Three consequences matter for state interconnection policy:

1. The order does not touch retail interconnection rules. States retain full
   authority over how a distribution utility processes an interconnection
   application, the queue in which it sits, and who pays for upgrades the
   application triggers. Federal Order 2222-B only governs what happens once
   a resource is already interconnected and wants to sell into wholesale
   markets.
2. Utilities report that 2222-B compliance filings have pulled engineering
   staff away from retail interconnection review, lengthening queues by an
   estimated 15% in the twelve months after a utility's compliance filing is
   accepted, though this figure is self-reported and not independently
   audited.
3. Aggregators now have a federal hook to demand faster retail
   interconnection, since a resource stuck in a retail queue cannot
   participate in the wholesale aggregation the order enables. This creates
   pressure states did not anticipate when 2222-B was issued.

Nothing in this memo should be read as a claim about wholesale market design;
that is out of scope for this report.
