# 2026 Multi-State Interconnection Timeline Survey (fictional)

Cascadia Energy Policy Institute surveyed twenty-two state energy offices in
Q1 2026 on distributed battery storage interconnection practice. Eighteen
responded.

Key figures:

- Median time from application to permission-to-operate for a residential
  battery energy storage system (BESS) under 25 kW: 61 days (range 12-190
  days across responding states).
- Median time for a commercial BESS between 25 kW and 1 MW: 148 days (range
  40-310 days).
- Fourteen of eighteen responding states use some version of a "fast track"
  process modeled on the original 2013 interconnection procedures, but only
  six have updated the fast-track size threshold since 2020 to account for
  larger residential batteries entering the market.
- Nine states report a queue position deposit requirement; of those, five
  report the deposit is non-refundable if a customer withdraws after a
  utility has begun a supplemental review, which several state staff
  described as a deterrent to speculative applications but also a barrier
  for lower-income applicants.
- Twelve states report having no statutory deadline for a utility's
  interconnection decision at all; six have a deadline but no penalty
  provision if the utility misses it.

Respondent free-text comments (paraphrased, not directly quotable):
utility engineering capacity, not policy design, was the most commonly cited
bottleneck, mentioned by eleven of eighteen respondents.
