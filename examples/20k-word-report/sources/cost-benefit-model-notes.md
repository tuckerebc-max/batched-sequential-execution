# Cost Allocation Modeling Notes (fictional, methodology memo)

This memo documents the assumptions behind the cost-allocation comparisons
used in Section 5 of the report. It is a methodology note, not a source of
headline statistics — treat any figure here as illustrative unless it also
appears in state-survey-2026.md or utility-interconnection-timelines.md.

Three cost-allocation methods are modeled:

1. **Applicant-pays** — the applicant bears 100% of any distribution upgrade
   triggered by their interconnection. Simplest to administer; concentrates
   cost on whichever applicant happens to trigger a threshold upgrade,
   sometimes called the "but-for" or "cost-causer" problem.
2. **Socialized-through-rates** — upgrade costs are added to the utility's
   rate base and recovered from all ratepayers. Removes the but-for problem
   but weakens the price signal that tells applicants where capacity is
   scarce.
3. **Hybrid threshold** — applicant pays up to a fixed dollar threshold per
   kW of nameplate capacity; costs above the threshold are socialized. This
   is the approach modeled in most of the model legislation drafted for
   Section 5.1.

The illustrative modeling in this memo assumes a distribution upgrade cost of
$180,000 per substation-level constraint resolved, spread across an average
of 40 applicants under the hybrid threshold method versus 1 applicant under
applicant-pays. These are modeling assumptions for sensitivity testing only
and must not be cited in the report body as observed data.
