# Case Study Notes: Two State Approaches (fictional)

## Vermont-style shared-savings queue reform

In this composite case, the state regulator authorized a "shared queue
priority" program in 2023: applicants who allow the utility to co-locate
their battery telemetry with a state-run monitoring platform move to the
front of the supplemental-review queue. Early results (18 months of data)
show program participants clearing supplemental review in a median of 51
days versus 90 days for non-participants, a $2,400 average avoided cost per
applicant in engineering re-review fees. Enrollment reached 61% of eligible
commercial applicants by month 18.

Tradeoff: the monitoring platform cost the state $3.1 million to build and
requires an ongoing $410,000 annual operating budget, funded through a small
per-application fee applied to all interconnection customers, participating
or not — a cross-subsidy some non-participants have objected to publicly.

## Hawaii-style capacity-mapping reform

In this composite case, the state required every distribution utility to
publish a public hosting-capacity map, updated quarterly, showing available
interconnection capacity by circuit. Applicants can self-screen before
filing. Utilities report a 22% reduction in withdrawn applications (fewer
customers filing where capacity was never available) and a 9% reduction in
median queue time for applications that do proceed, attributed to fewer
low-value engineering reviews of applications that would have failed anyway.

Tradeoff: two of four utilities in this composite required a rate case to
recover mapping-system costs, adding 14-20 months before the map went live.
