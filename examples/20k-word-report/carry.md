## Established facts
- Residential BESS median permission-to-operate: 61 days (range 12-190) [S2]; commercial BESS 25kW-1MW: 148 days (range 40-310) [S2].
- Four utilities' active queues range 640-4,780 applications; one grew 34% in a year [S3]. Supplemental review adds a median 90 days regardless of size [S3].
- 14/18 states use a 2013-era fast-track threshold; only 6/18 updated it since 2020 [S2]. 9/18 require a queue deposit; 5 of those make it non-refundable once supplemental review starts [S2].
- Federal Order 2222-B (2024, RTO compliance filings through 2026) governs wholesale-market aggregation only; it does not touch retail interconnection authority [S1]. Utilities self-report a ~15% queue lengthening from compliance-filing workload [S1].

## Interfaces/terms now defined
- "Interconnection queue" (Sec. 1): the retail-side application-to-permission process, entirely state/utility governed.
- "Supplemental review" (Sec. 1, mechanics deferred to 3.1): the engineering study triggered by a possible distribution upgrade; largest driver of delay variance.
- "Retail interconnection authority" (Sec. 2): the federal/state boundary — RTOs govern wholesale participation post-connection; states govern the queue itself.
