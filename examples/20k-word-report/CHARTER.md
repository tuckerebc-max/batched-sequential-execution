# CHARTER — Bridging the Interconnection Queue

## Deliverable
What: "Bridging the Interconnection Queue: State Policy Options for
Distributed Battery Storage, 2026-2030" — a policy research report.
Format: markdown, rendered last via the format skill (docx for state
legislative staff distribution).
Total size: 15800w
(body sections only, B002-B015; the executive summary and the
introduction/conclusion are front-matter.md / back-matter.md, written by
hand in the seam pass — see PLAN.md and WALKTHROUGH.md section 2 — and are
not separately budgeted plan units)

## Through-line
Interconnection queue delay, not cost or siting, is now the binding constraint on distributed
battery storage in the study states, and the policy options that clear it fastest are procedural
ones a legislature can enact without new appropriations.

## Audience
State energy office policy directors and legislative committee staff who
already know what interconnection is but do not track battery-specific
queue data across states. They will skim the executive summary and read one
or two case studies closely; assume no prior exposure to Federal Order
2222-B.

## Voice & register
Third person, policy-analyst register — precise, unadorned, comfortable with
numbers. No first person, no rhetorical questions, no advocacy language;
present tradeoffs, not verdicts.

## Non-negotiables
1. Every statistic, dollar figure, or year carries a [S#] source tag traceable
   to a file in Sources of truth.
2. The terminology lock is followed exactly; no synonyms for locked terms.
3. Model legislation is always presented as an option among options, never as
   a recommendation the report insists a state adopt.
4. No section contains concluding or summarizing language except the
   Executive Summary and the Conclusion.
5. Case studies are presented as composites with tradeoffs stated, not as
   uncomplicated success stories.

## Terminology lock
- rooftop solar -> distributed solar
- battery storage -> battery energy storage system (BESS)
- utility -> distribution utility
- microgrid -> community microgrid
- ISO/RTO -> regional transmission organization (RTO)

## Acceptance criteria
1. Body sections (B002-B015) total 15,800 words +/-20%; the finished
   deliverable (final.md, body plus the seam pass's front matter and back
   matter) runs somewhat longer than that and is not itself budget-checked.
2. Sections 3.1-3.3 describe interconnection queue mechanics consistent with
   [S1] and [S3]; no claim in those sections contradicts the source data.
3. Both case studies (4.1, 4.2) state at least one quantified tradeoff each,
   tagged [S4].
4. Section 5's cost-allocation comparison distinguishes observed data ([S2],
   [S3]) from illustrative modeling assumptions ([S5]) explicitly.
5. The Executive Summary is <=900 words and is comprehensible standing alone,
   without reading the rest of the report first.

## Out of scope
- Federal legislative process and any proposed changes to Federal Order
  2222-B itself.
- Wholesale electricity market design.
- Jurisdictions outside the United States.
- Net metering / retail rate design for exported energy (distinct policy
  question from interconnection queue processing).
